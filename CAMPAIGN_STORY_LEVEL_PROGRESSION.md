# Numberdroid — Campaign Story & Level Progression v0.1

Status: **binding v0.1 direction for story/level co-design**, not final level design. This document deliberately connects narrative beats to gameplay progression before exact maps, enemy rosters or new mechanics are locked.

Read `STORY_WORLD_FOUNDATION.md` first.

## Narrative convention

The numbered **story beat is primarily the result/end-state of the level**, not necessarily a cutscene before it. The level should build toward the beat through play.

Use this mental model:

```text
LEVEL FUNCTION
→ mechanic introduced / deepened / recombined
→ playable dramatic pressure
→ STORY BEAT / changed character state
```

Story information may also appear during a level through monitors, environmental storytelling, short radio lines and visible character actions. Do not force every beat into a long end-of-level cinematic.

## Intro — The Transfer

The player is still biologically human and about to undergo the Transfer into a first robot body.

The parents are proud and worried, but must show **different individual attitudes** toward the future. One may trust structure/PRIMUS more; the other may emphasise trying things and finding a personal direction. Neither should simply deliver the same exposition.

The Transfer itself should first feel positive and empowering.

Immediately afterward the parents' family phase ends and their work assignments reactivate. They are transferred away. The player interprets this as an abduction.

# Area I — Transfer Ship

Goal of the area: introduce the world, make robot embodiment exciting, establish PRIMUS/Kayo and turn the player's concern for the parents into active rebellion.

## Beat 1 — First body / safe exploration tutorial

**Level function**
- tutorial that cannot truly be failed,
- high focus on setting, movement, interaction and exploration,
- allow the player to enjoy simply being a robot,
- establish basic ship/world language without combat pressure.

**Story result**
The player experiences the positive side of the Transfer before the conflict dominates.

## Beat 2 — Assigned task versus parents

**Level function**
- the player is given a legitimate first task,
- the player abandons/resists it because finding the parents matters more,
- introduce stealth and environmental puzzle thinking,
- preferably **before body takeover is available**, so the player must solve problems with the current body.

**Story result**
The player chooses family over the role the system has assigned.

## Beat 3 — Kayo tries to classify the problem

**Level function**
- Kayo enters as the person responsible for assigning cores to useful tasks,
- he gives the player increasingly demanding or supposedly diagnostic tasks,
- pace becomes hectic,
- the tasks should reveal that the system is trying to find a slot for the player rather than asking what the player wants.

**Story result**
Kayo sees the player as a difficult assignment case; the player begins to see Kayo as part of the machine that took the parents away.

## Beat 4 — Rebellion / first simple body takeovers

**Level function**
- the player stops cooperating,
- introduce takeover/transfer through small, simple robots as an escape tool,
- use different small bodies to bypass restrictions rather than to dominate combat,
- increase pursuit and urgency.

**Story result**
The player turns the society's core technology against its intended use and becomes an active fugitive.

## Beat 5 — Escape from the ship

**Level function**
- reach an external/digital transmitter,
- a serious guard protects access,
- combine the first ship systems without becoming a giant difficulty spike,
- the player escapes through a transmitter to an uncertain destination rather than using normal authorised travel.

**Story result**
The player leaves the controlled Transfer environment and enters the wider world on their own terms.

# Area II — Deep Ocean

Goal of the area: deliver the first truly impossible destination, mix awe with danger, and move from hiding to capable body progression.

## Beat 6 — Find somewhere to hide

**Level function**
- small hostile robots/creatures in the environment,
- the player is still vulnerable,
- navigation is about avoiding danger and finding a safe hiding place, ideally a cave or protected natural structure.

**Story result**
For the first time the player is genuinely alone and has to create safety rather than expecting adults/the system to provide it.

## Beat 7 — Something enormous in the dark

**Level function**
- reveal only parts/silhouette of a truly large biological creature,
- major wow moment,
- smaller predators then create a fast escape sequence,
- explicitly shift from slow stealth to **speed and flight**,
- the environment is fascinating but the player has little time to study it.

**Story result**
Nature becomes simultaneously beautiful, alien and dangerous — not a museum display.

## Beat 8 — First classic small-to-large progression level

**Level function**
- a more recognisable Numberdroid level,
- begin with modest bodies/resources,
- defeat/outsmart encounters and progressively gain access to stronger/more capable bodies,
- let the player feel the core loop working cleanly.

**Story result**
The player begins to believe: “I can actually do this myself.”

## Beat 9 — Kayo manipulates the level

**Level function**
- reuse the now-understood progression grammar with a visible twist,
- Kayo is present/visible but unreachable,
- he changes doors, routes, robot availability or other authored systems in real time,
- the manipulation must be legible, not arbitrary invisible cheating.

**Story result**
The conflict becomes personal. Kayo can see the player succeeding outside his assignment and actively tries to force the situation back under control.

## Beat 10 — The father's trail / keys / transmitter search

Three-part dramatic structure:
1. the player discovers something that feels familiar from the parents,
2. the reveal: **this was the father's work / trail / presence**, confirming the player is following something real,
3. search for the next transmitter using the first meaningful key/access-gating system.

**Level function**
- introduce key/access logic cleanly,
- search and inference rather than simple corridor progress,
- end by finding the route onward.

**Story result**
The parents stop being an abstract destination. The player has concrete evidence of the father's path, but is still behind him.

# Area III — Volcanic / Extreme Industry

Goal of the area: turn body choice into a real toolset, introduce tactical planning, intensify comparison with Kayo, then deliver the painful meeting with the parents.

## Beat 11 — Several strong bodies, all needed

**Level function**
- multiple powerful bodies are available,
- no single one is “the best”,
- each clears a different part of the level,
- the player progressively opens the environment by deliberately switching between them.

**Story result**
The player experiences that capability is contextual: strength is useful, but different problems require different forms of competence.

## Beat 12 — Intelligent encounter order / first deliberate Joker use

**Binding design function, exact mechanic still open.**

**Level function**
- for the first time, taking enemies in an arbitrary order should make the level meaningfully harder,
- the player must read strengths/weaknesses and decide an intelligent sequence/body choice,
- this may eventually use a rock-paper-scissors-like relationship, but **do not lock the exact RPS implementation yet**,
- first deliberate tactical use of Joker abilities.

**Story result**
The player is no longer surviving by improvisation alone; judgement and planning begin to matter.

## Beat 13 — Shorter, harder competence test / Kayo contrast

**Level function**
- similar grammar to Beat 12 but harder and deliberately tiring,
- keep it shorter so “demanding” does not become exhausting,
- require cleaner planning and more deliberate use of abilities.

**Story result**
After the player struggles through it, show Kayo accomplishing comparable work almost effortlessly and with a broader ability set. His arrogance has real foundations.

## Beat 14 — Direct competition

**Level function**
- direct race/competition against another robot,
- the player must win through actual play rather than a scripted result,
- the challenge should test the skills learned so far rather than introduce a large unrelated system.

**Story result**
Kayo is forced to acknowledge the player's competence. This is not yet friendship, but the player can no longer be dismissed as merely unruly or incapable.

## Beat 15 — Combined challenge / the parents are finally reached

**Level function**
- difficult level combining the newly established tactical elements,
- boss/gate at the end,
- only after defeating it can the player enter the room/work area where the parents are.

**Story result**
The player finally reaches the parents — and is disappointed. They were not simply prisoners waiting to be rescued. They care about the player, but they also value their work and expect the player to start living independently.

The conversation goes badly. The player's accusation that they “only want to work” is unfair but also hits a real weakness.

The player leaves through another transmitter feeling hurt rather than triumphant.

# Area IV — Moon / Vacuum

Goal of the area: give the player freedom and growing mastery, introduce optional self-directed progression, reverse earlier predator dynamics and break Kayo's assignment system.

## Beat 16 — Freedom and lunar exploration

**Level function**
- deliberately easier after Beat 15,
- broad player freedom,
- optional environmental puzzles and places to explore,
- strong lunar/vacuum wonder moments,
- first optional Treasure Golem / Beutedroide encounter is a good candidate here.

**Story result**
The player has enormous freedom but no externally supplied answer to what to do with it.

## Beat 17 — Optional bonus core conversion / self-directed strengthening

**Binding design function, exact system still open.**

**Level function**
- the player discovers a lunar resource/technology that can provide **bonus core conversion or optional strengthening beyond the minimum progression path**,
- this should reward curiosity and intentional investment,
- exact economy, numbers and persistence are not yet locked.

**Story result**
The player begins to shape capability voluntarily rather than merely taking whatever body the current obstacle requires.

## Beat 18 — The hunter becomes prey

**Level function**
- the player is pursued again,
- use enemies/archetypes that previously caused fear or forced escape,
- the player has become strong/skilled enough to defeat them now,
- this should feel like earned mastery rather than simple stat inflation.

**Story result**
The player recognises their own growth. Earlier rules of fear no longer apply.

## Beat 19 — Moon central station in dangerous chaos

**Level function**
- arrive at the central operational station,
- systems are failing and the environment is visibly dangerous,
- Kayo's coordination/assignment structure is overloaded,
- people/robots wait for instructions and cascading failures expose how dependent everything has become on him.

**Story result**
Kayo initially sees proof that he is indispensable, then realises that this dependency is itself his failure.

## Beat 20 — Fire / rescue / Kayo turns

**Level function**
- the station is burning,
- visually spectacular dynamic fire light, alarms and warning lamps are desirable,
- the player must act intelligently and carefully rather than simply rush toward the exit,
- prioritise routes/actions and actually extinguish or contain the disaster,
- the player succeeds where overloaded Kayo could not and **saves Kayo as well**.

**Story result**
The player does not abandon the former rival. Kayo experiences competent help that is not a contest for status.

Kayo's transformation becomes active: problems need a team, and his job must become making others capable rather than making them dependent.

They travel together to the Bio-Ark to report directly to PRIMUS.

# Area V — Bio-Ark / PRIMUS

Goal of the area: make biological complexity into gameplay, allow PRIMUS to present the strongest version of the order argument, then let the player demonstrate self-organising balance rather than merely win an argument.

## Beat 21 — Biological systems introduction

**Level function**
- introduce the Bio-Ark and first bio-themed gameplay elements in an accessible form,
- PRIMUS is primarily present through monitors/displays rather than physically everywhere,
- PRIMUS comments on the player, Kayo and the environment from an order/management perspective.

**Story result**
The player finally encounters PRIMUS's worldview directly in the place PRIMUS considers messy and management-intensive.

## Beat 22 — Biodiversity in depth

**Level function**
- harder versions of the new biological systems,
- multiple species/processes influence one another,
- make biodiversity visually and mechanically fascinating rather than educational exposition,
- allow a quieter wonder moment where possible.

**Story result**
The player begins to see that living complexity has patterns and relationships that are not obvious from a single component.

## Beat 23 — Nature appears to go out of control

**Level function**
- the environment seems increasingly unstable,
- level layout and biological mechanics start becoming one system rather than “map plus gimmick”,
- routes, hazards and opportunities change through biological interactions.

**Story result**
PRIMUS's argument appears strongest: without intervention this looks like chaos.

## Beat 24 — Old and new systems recombined

**Level function**
- combine established robot/body/tactical mechanics with the biological systems,
- prepare the player to reason about interconnected consequences rather than just defeat individual blockers,
- this is the synthesis level before the final demonstration.

**Story result**
The player has enough understanding of both machine control and biological interaction to challenge PRIMUS on PRIMUS's own terms.

## Beat 25 — Demonstrate balance

**Level function**
- the player must identify/collect/remove several elements that appear to be causing the chaos,
- crucially, removing one factor causes a visible **counter-reaction elsewhere**,
- the puzzle reveals that the components are connected; “remove every bad thing” is not the solution,
- the player must understand and demonstrate a dynamic balance rather than force a static perfect state.

Exact ecological mechanics remain to be designed after the first visual/story pass.

**Story result**
The player shows PRIMUS that the system can respond, compensate and reorganise without one manager assigning every component its place.

PRIMUS discovers a form of order beyond central control.

The deeper final step is personal: PRIMUS becomes genuinely curious about a living process and realises that passion itself was never something PRIMUS could assign or calculate.

# Outro — A place that did not exist

## Player

The player still does not fit a clean existing assignment.

This is no longer treated as failure.

Kayo helps the player define/create a new role rather than assigning an old one. Working concept: Pathfinder / Grenzgänger / Explorer — someone who reaches new environments, tries bodies, connects skills and prepares paths for others.

## Kayo

Kayo keeps the valuable core of his profession but changes its purpose.

Old question:

> “Where should I assign you?”

New question:

> **“What do you want to become, and what do we need to learn or build to get you there?”**

Helping the player create a role that did not exist completes his transformation from assigner to enabler.

## Parents

The parents may continue work they genuinely like. Their change is not “work is bad”. They consciously make more room for their child and for their relationship instead of treating family as a completed phase or using enjoyable work as the default excuse for having no time.

## PRIMUS

PRIMUS uses extraordinary organisational ability in support of biological preservation/study without controlling people's identities. What may begin as punishment becomes PRIMUS's first self-discovered passion.

A suitable final comic image: PRIMUS patiently watches an unpredictable small animal, admits “I do not know” what it will do next, and finds that fascinating.

# Progression guardrails

- Do not lock every proposed new mechanic from this document immediately; the document defines **what the level needs to teach/feel**, not always the final implementation.
- Beat 12's enemy-order relationship is intentionally not yet a fixed RPS system.
- Beat 17's optional strengthening/core-conversion economy is intentionally not yet specified.
- Treasure Golem/Beutedroide remains future implementation; Beat 16 is a proposed narrative/gameplay introduction point.
- Story beats should remain short and playable. Use environmental storytelling, monitor dialogue and in-motion moments instead of frequent long cutscenes.
- Each area should have a strong visual identity while sharing one coherent Numberdroid/PRIMUS design language.

# Immediate art-direction slice

The next visual design milestone should focus on **Intro + Beats 1–5 / Transfer Ship**.

It must answer:
- what biological childhood looks like in this machine society,
- what the Transfer looks and feels like,
- what the player's first body looks like,
- how PRIMUS order is expressed through architecture, signage and UI,
- how Kayo's status/competence is visually communicated,
- how human/family traces differ from machine infrastructure,
- how to avoid a generic sci-fi spaceship look.

Do not produce final art for all 25 beats yet.
