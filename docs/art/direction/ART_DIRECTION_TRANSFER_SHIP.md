# Numberdroid — Transfer Ship Art Direction v0.1

Status: **approved first art-direction hypothesis for visual exploration**  
Scope: **Intro + Beats 1–5 / Transfer Ship only**  
Branch: `agent/transfer-ship-art-direction`  
Base: current canonical `main`

This document translates the binding story/world foundation into a first bounded visual language. It is deliberately not a final campaign-wide style guide. The purpose of the first graphic push is to discover what Numberdroid looks like before large-scale asset production.

## 1. Visual thesis

Numberdroid is not primarily a story about robots. It is about a person who is no longer bound to one body while society simultaneously tries to bind that person to one role.

The first visual language therefore grows from two opposing ideas:

- **CORE** — the persistent person/self that can move between bodies.
- **SLOT** — the place, role, body, workstation or path that PRIMUS has already prepared and assigned.

Working visual shorthand:

```text
CORE:        ●
SLOT:      [ ● ]
```

The resulting core motif is:

> **The player can move between bodies. PRIMUS believes every core belongs in the correct slot.**

This must be visible before the story explains it.

## 2. CORE — visual identity of the person

The Core should be:

- small relative to a robot body,
- visually precious,
- clearly persistent across body changes,
- more personal than the surrounding machine infrastructure,
- readable at a glance during Transfer sequences,
- capable of carrying an individual light/signature.

For the player, the Core should use a warm personal accent that remains recognizable through different bodies.

The body changes. The Core does not.

## 3. SLOT — PRIMUS visual language

PRIMUS order should not be expressed primarily through dark military sci-fi, prison imagery or villain decoration.

The world should feel safe, competent and functional.

Recurring visual forms:

- brackets / frames / open clamps,
- docking recesses,
- registration marks,
- alignment lines,
- body stations,
- clearly bounded work positions,
- guided paths,
- fit-confirmation lights,
- nested rectangular or rounded-rectangular systems,
- circles/cores placed into larger ordered structures.

The motif should recur in:

- doors,
- floors,
- Transfer machinery,
- robot docks,
- signage,
- embedded interfaces,
- access systems,
- workstations,
- Kayo's body language.

Avoid generic "sci-fi decoration" without a function.

## 4. Transfer Ship architecture

The Transfer Ship is a **civilian machine society**, not a warship.

Desired qualities:

- optimistic and highly maintained,
- broad circulation space for different robot body sizes,
- calm large surfaces rather than continuous machinery noise,
- visible functional structure,
- architecture that guides bodies into intended positions,
- clear hierarchy of public, operational and restricted spaces,
- technology integrated into the architecture rather than scattered as random props.

### Form language

Primary:
- large soft/rounded architectural masses,
- precise inset technical frames,
- strong horizontal/vertical organization,
- circular Core elements nested inside bracket/slot structures.

Avoid:
- ubiquitous hexagons,
- arbitrary 45-degree panels,
- random exposed pipes,
- excessive vents,
- military bulkheads everywhere,
- "dark metal + cyan neon + hazard stripes" as the main identity.

## 5. Biological childhood / family traces

The contrast is **not nature versus technology** and not wood versus metal.

Human/family presence is shown through things that do not need to be optimized:

- a bag left slightly out of place,
- clothes or fabric,
- a child drawing,
- a repaired or customized personal object,
- mismatched drinking containers,
- a keepsake with no functional value,
- slight asymmetry or clutter,
- personalized color accents.

The strongest contrast:

> PRIMUS gives everything a place. Family life leaves traces that matter without needing an assigned purpose.

## 6. TRANSFER — emotional and visual sequence

The first Transfer must not begin as horror.

It is a positive rite of passage:

- adulthood,
- independence,
- access to the wider world,
- new senses,
- new physical possibility.

Desired visual progression:

1. The biological child enters the Transfer frame/cradle.
2. Parents remain visibly present and emotionally distinct.
3. The system identifies/extracts the persistent Core.
4. The player sees that the Core can inhabit another body.
5. The Core moves visibly into the first PICO body.
6. PICO activates; the player experiences empowerment first.
7. Only afterward does PRIMUS perform the routine administrative consequence: family phase complete, parents reassigned/transferred away.

Important dramatic rule:

> The same elegant machine that gives the child freedom also appears to take the parents away.

Do not visually signal "evil machine" before this reversal.

## 7. First player body — PICO

PICO must not look like a baby robot or a deliberately weak starter body.

Desired read:

- compact,
- capable,
- robust,
- highly controllable,
- general-purpose,
- clearly less specialized than later bodies.

Visual principle:

> PICO looks like **possibility**, not deficiency.

Possible design traits:

- visible Core housing,
- neutral modular/tool interfaces,
- few permanently specialized attachments,
- confident broad silhouette,
- simple readable locomotion,
- design that can plausibly enter many environments before specialized bodies become necessary.

The existing gameplay identity remains binding: balanced drive feel, standard physical body size, no current special duel ability.

## 8. Utility robot

The normal worker/utility robot exists to show how the society works when nothing is wrong.

It should:

- look clearly purpose-built,
- visibly belong to a job category,
- occupy work slots naturally,
- appear useful rather than threatening,
- provide silhouette contrast with PICO,
- reinforce that most of the robot population is simply working.

This is an important visual counterweight to later guards and pursuit.

## 9. Kayo

Kayo is not a secretly mediocre villain and should not look like a generic black elite robot.

Desired visual read:

- genuinely exceptional manufacture,
- precise,
- complete,
- high-status,
- elegant rather than bulky,
- many functions integrated cleanly,
- perfect PRIMUS compatibility.

Kayo should maximize the SLOT language:

- precise alignment features,
- seamless interface points,
- status authorization integrated into the body,
- very controlled proportions,
- minimal improvisation,
- premium surface treatment.

He should look like someone who fits this world perfectly.

The contrast with the player is not simply stronger/weaker:

> **Kayo is already specialized and validated. PICO is still open.**

## 10. Palette and lighting logic

Exact production colors remain open. The first slice should test this hierarchy.

### PRIMUS / infrastructure

- light warm off-white / ceramic grey primary surfaces,
- graphite or dark mineral recesses,
- restrained mint/cyan normal-status light,
- tightly controlled system accents,
- bright, calm, legible public spaces.

### Family / human trace

- warmer amber,
- subdued coral / terracotta,
- fabrics and non-system color variation,
- localized warm pools rather than global color grading.

### Player Core

- recognizable warm personal glow that persists across bodies.

### Kayo

- cleaner/premium version of system materials,
- rare precise status accent,
- avoid simply adding more neon.

### Escalation through Beats 1–5

Do not replace the environment with "red evil mode".

As pressure rises:

- route guidance becomes command guidance,
- doors close,
- directional light becomes stronger,
- status lines become warning/containment lines,
- warm family traces disappear from the player's route,
- amber warning states increase,
- the same ordered architecture begins to feel controlling.

## 11. Small props and environmental vocabulary

The first slice should use a deliberately small reusable prop set.

Candidate props:

1. Core cradle / body dock.
2. Compact civilian storage unit.
3. Family bench or waiting module with personal objects.
4. PRIMUS route/signage pylon or wall strip.
5. Utility service console.
6. Charging / diagnostic socket.
7. Personal keepsake / drawing attachment point.
8. Floor alignment marker / body parking frame.

Each prop must support either function, story or visual grammar. Avoid filler clutter.

## 12. Technical production constraints from current runtime

The existing architecture is sufficient for this art experiment and must not be replaced.

Current relevant constraints:

- orthogonal 64 px tile system,
- multiple visible tile layers,
- separate collision/walkable/object data,
- separate DOM-layer robots, doors, labels and interactables,
- standard deck robot sprites around 50–52 px,
- large bodies around 96 px,
- local RAF movement/camera must remain untouched,
- current Floor/Tiled content model remains authoritative.

Consequences for art:

- silhouette is more important than micro-detail,
- floor/wall shapes need strong large-scale readability,
- status colors must survive at small scale,
- robots require distinct mass distribution and outer contour,
- the visual slice must be evaluated in actual top-down gameplay scale, not only as concept art.

## 13. First bounded art-direction block — TS-01 Transfer Hall

### Scope

One characteristic Transfer Ship room, approximately **12 × 8 64-px tiles** as the initial target footprint.

One continuous composition contains three conceptual zones:

```text
FAMILY NICHE
→ TRANSFER CRADLE
→ MACHINE / SOCIETY EXIT
```

### Required visual elements

- biological child / family trace,
- two parents as distinct presences,
- Transfer element,
- visible Core,
- PICO first body,
- one ordinary utility/work robot,
- Kayo/status body,
- PRIMUS signage/UI,
- one standard door/threshold,
- 5–6 reusable props,
- first material palette,
- first lighting hierarchy.

### Three states the same room must support

1. **Before Transfer** — warm, anticipatory, family present.
2. **During Transfer** — positive visual wow moment; Core/body identity becomes clear.
3. **Immediately after Transfer** — player now inhabits PICO; parents are reassigned through the same visual system.

The room is successful only if these emotional states come from staging/light/system behavior rather than three unrelated art sets.

## 14. First evaluation deliverables

Before production asset expansion, evaluate four tightly scoped artifacts:

1. **Transfer Hall style frame**  
   Architecture, materials, Transfer, family/system contrast, lighting.

2. **Top-down gameplay frame**  
   Same language at realistic 64-px tile / ~52-px standard-body scale.

3. **Body lineup**  
   PICO / utility robot / Kayo; large concept read plus gameplay silhouette check.

4. **PRIMUS mini-language sheet**  
   CORE & SLOT motif, signage, 4–6 UI/environment symbols, material/palette swatches.

Do not expand to full Beat 1–5 asset production until these four artifacts feel like one recognizable Numberdroid world.

## 15. Acceptance questions

The first slice should be judged against these questions:

- Does the world look desirable before it becomes controlling?
- Can the Transfer feel empowering rather than horrific?
- Is the persistent Core/self visually obvious?
- Is PRIMUS order visible without villain clichés?
- Does CORE & SLOT read as a repeatable visual grammar?
- Does family/human presence feel personal without becoming a separate rustic art style?
- Does PICO look open/general-purpose rather than weak?
- Does Kayo look genuinely competent and high-status?
- Can PICO, utility robot and Kayo be distinguished at gameplay scale?
- Does the room avoid generic sci-fi at first glance?
- Can the style be built with the existing Floor/Tiled + sprite runtime without architectural changes?

## 16. Explicit non-goals for this block

Do not yet:

- produce all five Transfer Ship levels,
- make final Intro cinematics/dialogue,
- redesign the entire robot catalog,
- create final art for Deep Ocean / Volcanic / Moon / Bio-Ark,
- change movement/rendering architecture,
- create a new Tiled/runtime abstraction,
- lock campaign-wide production scope,
- treat current concept images as production assets before gameplay-scale validation.

The next decision point is visual, not architectural.
